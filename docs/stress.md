# Stress
Below is the **Updated Social Readjustment Rating Scale (2023)**, reproduced from Wallace, Cooper, Sel & Russo's modernisation of the original scale. The original was devised in 1967 by Holmes and Rahe. The updated scale keeps all 43 of the original life events (with the wording of 12 items modernised) and adds one new item — *"Single person, living alone"* — for 44 items in total. Marriage is the fixed anchor at 50.

The values below are the **mean weights (Life Change Units, LCUs)** reported in the paper's Table 8, listed in the updated rank order.

## How to interpret a total score

You tally the LCUs for every event you have experienced in roughly the past 12 months. Holmes and Rahe's original interpretive guidance — which the 2023 paper references but does **not** replace with new thresholds — was:

- **Below 150** — low life-change stress; the respondent was likely to stay healthy over the next year.
- **150–299** — moderate stress; an elevated (~50%) chance of a stress-related health problem in the near future.
- **300 and above** — high stress; those who fell ill typically scored in this range (~80% chance).

> **Caveat:** the modernised weights are on average ~28% higher than the 1967 values, so total scores now run higher than they did on the original scale. The classic 150 / 300 cut-offs should therefore be treated as a rough guide rather than a validated threshold for the updated scale.
